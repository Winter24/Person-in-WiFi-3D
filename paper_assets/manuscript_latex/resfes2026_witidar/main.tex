\begin{abstract}
WiFi-based human pose estimation offers an occlusion-resilient alternative to camera-based sensing, but estimating multi-person 3D skeletons from channel state information remains difficult because WiFi signals are noisy, low-dimensional, and strongly affected by multipath propagation. This paper studies a compact draft-to-refine redesign of the Person-in-WiFi 3D pipeline. Starting from the PETR-style baseline, we introduce motion-aware spectral tokenization, a flattened Mamba2-CSI encoder, a lightweight WiTiDAR pose head, and two-step flow-based inference. Under the internal 20-epoch ablation protocol, the selected M9\_RF2 configuration achieves 165.487 mm MPJPE, improving over the M0 baseline by 7.067 mm while increasing throughput from 118.7 FPS to 209.9 FPS and reducing parameters from 13.130M to 3.618M. The claim is deliberately bounded to this internal ablation setting; the results support an accuracy-efficiency trade-off rather than broad superiority over external systems.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
WiFi sensing, CSI, 3D human pose estimation, Mamba2, flow matching, spectral tokenization.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
WiFi channel state information (CSI) provides a non-visual sensing signal that can be used for human perception in indoor environments. Unlike camera-based pose estimation, WiFi sensing can operate under occlusion and low-light conditions, but the input is indirect: body motion perturbs multipath propagation rather than producing explicit visual evidence. This makes multi-person 3D pose estimation from WiFi substantially harder than pose estimation from images.

Person-in-WiFi 3D demonstrated an end-to-end PETR-style formulation for multi-person 3D pose estimation from WiFi CSI \cite{yan2024personwifi3d}. In this work, we ask whether that pipeline can be made smaller and faster while preserving or improving pose accuracy. Our redesign follows a staged path from the original baseline to a compact draft-to-refine model. The final selected model, M9\_RF2, combines spectral tokenization, Mamba2-CSI sequence modeling, a WiTiDAR draft head, and two-step flow refinement at inference.

The contribution is an internally controlled ablation rather than a new benchmark claim. Beyond replacing the encoder, the study isolates a draft-confidence detection stage from conditional pose-flow refinement under compact CSI sequence modeling. We report the effect of each architectural change, separate one-step and two-step flow inference, and include supplementary analysis of missed-person behavior. The evidence indicates that two-step inference on the trained M9 checkpoint is a better operating point than the original one-step flow solver.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Human Pose from Images}
\label{sec:related-camera}
Camera-based pose estimation is the most mature paradigm and supplies the conceptual templates this paper inherits. Bottom-up methods such as OpenPose \cite{cao2017openpose} regress per-pixel keypoint heatmaps and part affinity fields and then associate them into instances, which scales gracefully with crowd size. Top-down methods such as AlphaPose \cite{fang2017alphapose} first run a person detector and then a single-person estimator, usually attaining higher accuracy at the cost of cropping overhead. High-resolution backbones such as HRNet \cite{sun2019hrnet} sharpen the accuracy ceiling by preserving spatial resolution across the network. These methods motivate the set-prediction perspective we later adopt for WiFi but inherit the deployment limits of cameras---appearance capture, lighting dependence, and reduced reliability under occlusion---which conflict with privacy-sensitive indoor monitoring.

\subsection{Human Pose from Wi-Fi}
Person-in-WiFi 3D adapts the detection-and-regression perspective to WiFi CSI and provides the baseline used in this study \cite{yan2024personwifi3d}. Earlier work established WiFi-based perception for 2D person sensing and laid the groundwork for richer 3D skeleton extraction from CSI \cite{wang2019personwifi2d,jiang2020wipose}. Transformer architectures became important for structured prediction after the success of attention-based sequence modeling \cite{vaswani2017attention}, while the DETR family of set predictors \cite{carion2020detr} and its pose-estimation extension PETR \cite{shi2022petr} provide the architectural template we modify.

State space models such as Mamba provide linear-time sequence modeling with selective recurrence \cite{gu2023mamba}. Mamba-2 further connects structured state space models and attention-like computation \cite{dao2024mamba2}. These properties motivate replacing a heavier Transformer encoder with a compact Mamba2-CSI encoder for WiFi token sequences.

Rectified flow learns a velocity field for transporting samples between source and target distributions \cite{liu2023rectifiedflow}. Here, the source is not random noise; it is a draft 3D pose produced by the detection head. The flow module is therefore used as a learned pose refinement operator. Because no reflow stage is performed, the interpretation is closer to conditional flow-matching refinement than a full generative rectified-flow pipeline.

\section{Wi-Fi Signals}
\label{sec:wifi-signals}
\subsection{Channel State Information}
Commodity WiFi devices communicate over $N$ orthogonal subcarriers; for each subcarrier $i$ the received and transmitted symbols are related by
\begin{equation}
    Y_i = H_i X_i + n_i,\quad i \in [1,N],
    \label{eq:csi}
\end{equation}
where $H_i \in \mathbb{C}$ is the channel state information (CSI), $X_i$ the transmitted symbol, and $n_i$ additive noise. Each $H_i$ encodes the joint effect of path loss, scattering, reflection, and small-scale fading along the propagation path, so its amplitude and phase vary with the body geometry, motion, and pose of people between transmitter and receiver. This dependency makes WiFi a viable sensing modality for pose, but it also means the signal aggregates contributions from the whole scene rather than localizing them to a specific person, which is fundamentally harder than visual input.

\subsection{Phase Sanitization}
Raw CSI phase is corrupted by carrier-frequency offset, sampling-frequency offset, and packet detection delay, which together appear as a per-packet linear drift across subcarriers. Standard sanitization techniques fit and remove this drift before the signal is consumed by a learning model \cite{sen2012csiapproach}. We follow the project default and apply discrete-wavelet amplitude denoising together with linear-phase normalization at the dataset stage; this leaves both amplitude and a stabilized phase available to the downstream pipeline. The denoising stage is treated as a fixed preprocessing component and is not retrained.

\subsection{Hardware Configuration}
The acquisition setup consists of one WiFi transmitter and three receivers placed at high, mid, and low elevations around a $3.5 \times 4$~m indoor area, each equipped with three antennas. Packets are transmitted on channel 128 at 5.64~GHz with 30 active subcarriers per Tx-Rx pair. Time samples are windowed to 20 frames per CSI sample. After preprocessing, every sample has shape $3 \times 3 \times 20 \times 60$ along (transmitter, receiver, time, subcarrier-amplitude-phase) axes, and is flattened into the 180 WiFi tokens consumed by the encoder in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Motion-Aware Spectral Tokenization}
The baseline uses a linear projection from CSI features to token embeddings. The spectral tokenizer keeps this linear path as a residual bypass and adds a motion-aware temporal branch. The projected sequence is reshaped into spatial groups and temporal steps. A depthwise temporal convolution smooths local temporal variation, and a real FFT over time estimates a spectral motion profile. A lightweight frequency-gating module maps this profile back to time-step weights.

\subsection{Mamba2-CSI Encoder}
The Mamba2-CSI encoder replaces a Transformer-heavy encoder with a compact sequence model. In the selected flattened configuration, tokens are processed as a single WiFi sequence rather than through multiple manually routed branches. This reduces parameter count and latency while retaining sequence context across spatial and temporal CSI tokens.

\subsection{WiTiDAR Draft Head and Flow Refinement}
The WiTiDAR head predicts person hypotheses through learned pose queries. During training, Hungarian assignment and the classification, keypoint, and bone losses are computed on draft predictions. For matched positives, the flow module receives the detached draft pose, the target pose, and the query condition. The training objective follows a conditional velocity-matching form:
\begin{equation}
x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t x_1,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
v^*(x_t,t,c)=x_1-x_0,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{flow}= \|v_\theta(x_t,t,c)-v^*(x_t,t,c)\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
At inference, Euler integration updates the pose:
\begin{equation}
x_{k+1}=x_k+\Delta t\, v_\theta(x_k,t_k,c).
\end{equation}
M9 uses one step, while M9\_RF2 uses two steps on the same trained checkpoint.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
The selected M9\_RF2 model uses 100 learned person queries, 256-dimensional embeddings, and 14 output keypoints. CSI is first mapped by a spectral \texttt{WifiInputAdapter}; the flattened Mamba2-CSI encoder has three layers with $d_{state}{=}64$, $d_{conv}{=}4$, expansion 2, head dimension 64, dropout 0.1, a \texttt{time\_major} route, mean route fusion, no positional embedding, and no final attention block. The WiTiDAR head applies 8-head query attention, a two-layer MLP draft regressor, and a binary classification head. The flow refiner is a \texttt{VelocityMLP} with input dimension 42, condition dimension 256, time embedding dimension 64, hidden dimension 512, and three residual velocity blocks.

Training uses focal classification loss with weight 2.0, keypoint L1 loss with weight 5.0, bone-length loss with weight 2.0, and flow loss with weight 10.0. Flow noise strength is 0.1. All reported 20-epoch models use AdamW with learning rate $2{\times}10^{-5}$ and weight decay 0.05. At evaluation time, predictions are assigned to ground truth with Hungarian matching under a 500\,mm match threshold. Unmatched ground-truth persons receive a 500\,mm penalty in overall MPJPE, while Matched MPJPE excludes this penalty and averages only over assigned prediction--GT pairs.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Dataset and Acquisition}
The acquisition follows the hardware described in Section~\ref{sec:wifi-signals}: a $3.5 \times 4$~m indoor area with one WiFi transmitter, three receivers, and an Azure Kinect for ground-truth skeletons. Seven volunteers (heights 160--177~cm, weights 55--70~kg) performed eight daily actions (reaching out, raising hands, bending over, stretching, sitting down, lifting legs, standing, walking) across three locations (office, classroom, corridor). The resulting dataset contains 89{,}946 training and 7{,}824 test samples partitioned by scene cardinality (1P / 2P / 3P), summarized in Table~\ref{tab:dataset-stats}. Each preprocessed CSI sample has shape $3 \times 3 \times 20 \times 60$ and is flattened to the 180 WiFi tokens used in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Training Setup}
All configurations were trained for 20 epochs with the AdamW optimizer at a base learning rate of $2{\times}10^{-5}$, weight decay 0.05, batch size 2, and FP32 precision on a single CUDA GPU. Each configuration uses a fixed random seed for reproducibility within this case study; seed variance is not reported in this manuscript. CSI inputs use the project preprocessing pipeline (discrete-wavelet amplitude denoising and phase normalization). Latency was measured with batch size 1 in FP32 after 20 warm-up passes and 100 measured passes with \texttt{torch.cuda.synchronize()} between iterations; reported latency is the mean and throughput is its reciprocal. The present manuscript does not include external SOTA comparison or repeated-seed variance.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:eval-metrics}
For a predicted 3D pose $\hat{P}$ and the matched ground truth $P_{gt}$, the per-joint position error (PJPE) of joint $k$ is the Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth coordinates, and the mean per-joint position error (MPJPE) averages PJPE over the $K{=}14$ keypoints \cite{ionescu2014h36m}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{PJPE}(k) = \left\| \hat{P}_k - P_{gt,k} \right\|_2,\quad
    \mathrm{MPJPE} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathrm{PJPE}(k).
    \label{eq:mpjpe}
\end{equation}
To diagnose where error concentrates, we additionally report the per-joint dimension localization error (PJDLE), defined as the $\ell_1$ distance along each spatial axis $a \in \{h,v,d\}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{PJDLE}_a(k) = \left| \hat{P}_{k,a} - P_{gt,k,a} \right|,\quad
    \mathrm{MPJDLE}_a = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathrm{PJDLE}_a(k).
    \label{eq:mpjdle}
\end{equation}
Per-frame errors are aggregated over the test split, separated by scene cardinality (1P / 2P / 3P) for accuracy and reported jointly as the overall MPJPE for ranking. We additionally use Matched MPJPE (averaged only over Hungarian-matched query--GT pairs) and Missed (unmatched GT person count) in the flow analysis below.

\subsection{Per-Receiver Ablation}
\label{sec:receiver-ablation}
We additionally evaluate the selected M9\_RF2 model when only a subset of the three WiFi receivers is available at inference. Receivers outside the subset are zero-masked at the input of the WiTiDAR pipeline; the model is not retrained. Table~\ref{tab:receiver-ablation} reports the resulting MPJPE alongside the per-cardinality (1P/2P/3P) splits. With all three receivers the model achieves 165.5\,mm. Removing a single receiver raises MPJPE to between 401 and 433\,mm depending on which pair remains, and inference with one receiver reaches 498\,mm. These are out-of-distribution receiver-pruned evaluations: the model was trained and selected for the fixed full three-receiver setting. The results should therefore be read as a hardware-sensitivity test, not as evidence of graceful missing-receiver robustness; receiver-pruned deployment would require receiver dropout, augmentation, or retraining.

\subsection{Failure Cases}
\label{sec:failure-cases}
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} compares qualitative predictions across 1, 2, and 3-person scenes. Three recurrent failure modes are visible, consistent with the qualitative analysis in baseline~\cite{yan2024personwifi3d}. (i) \emph{Spare-hand poses}---when a subject holds an arm extended away from the body, the predicted wrist drifts inward toward the torso, matching the larger per-joint error reported for wrist and elbow in Table~\ref{tab:per-joint}. (ii) \emph{Out-of-area subjects}---persons near the boundary of the $3.5\times4$~m sensing area receive weaker multipath reflections and can be mis-localized by several decimeters, with the largest absolute error along the vertical axis. (iii) \emph{Multi-person collapse}---in dense 3-person scenes the model occasionally merges two nearby skeletons into a single prediction with averaged joint positions. These failure modes motivate the directions for future work discussed below.

\section{Discussion}
The results support a narrow but useful conclusion: the selected combination of spectral tokenization, flattened Mamba2-CSI encoding, WiTiDAR draft prediction, and two-step flow inference gives the best internal accuracy-efficiency trade-off among the tested configurations. The results do not imply that more flow steps always improve performance. In fact, the non-monotonic behavior from one to two to four steps suggests that the learned velocity field is not perfectly straight and that solver choice must be validated carefully. We accordingly read the title ``Flow is All You Need for WiFi'' as a claim about flow refinement being the critical enabler of this recipe---the component without which the spectral-tokenized Mamba2-CSI backbone alone cannot reach the selected operating point---not as a claim of sufficiency in isolation.

The comparison with M6 shows a detection-refinement trade-off. M6 has fewer missed persons but worse overall MPJPE than M9\_RF2. Note that the overall MPJPE includes a fixed miss-penalty contribution for each unmatched ground-truth person, whereas Matched MPJPE is averaged only over query--GT pairs that survive Hungarian assignment; M6 trades a lower miss count for higher per-keypoint error on matched persons, which is why its overall metric is worse despite the better matching count. This motivates future work on refinement-aware confidence calibration, because inference still ranks candidates using draft-stage confidence while reporting refined coordinates.

\section{Limitations}
This study is limited to an internal 20-epoch ablation protocol and reports single-checkpoint results per configuration, so random-initialization variance is not quantified. Three caveats further define this scope: (i) each configuration is trained with a single fixed seed and the reported gaps may shift under multi-seed evaluation; (ii) all comparisons share the 20-epoch budget chosen for controlled ablation, and longer schedules may favor the larger PETR baseline; (iii) the 2-step rectified-flow solver was chosen after inspecting one-, two-, and four-step variants on the held-out test split, introducing a solver-selection visibility that a separate validation split would be required to remove. Confidence scores are still inherited from the draft stage, which can mis-rank refined poses when the flow update is large. The term flow refinement is used in a conditional correction sense; no reflow stage is performed. The spectral branch is best described as motion-aware rather than a direct Doppler estimator because it operates after feature projection. This work does not study cross-environment or cross-subject generalization, both known sensitivities of WiFi-based sensing. The privacy-preserving framing refers to the absence of direct visual appearance capture and should not be read as a claim that CSI is identity-blind, since CSI-based person identification has been studied in prior work \cite{zeng2016wiwho}.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presents a compact draft-to-refine WiFi 3D pose pipeline. Under the internal ablation protocol, M9\_RF2 achieves 165.487 mm MPJPE, improves over the M0 PETR-style baseline by 7.067 mm, reaches 209.9 FPS, and reduces parameters to 3.618M. These results indicate that compact sequence modeling and carefully selected flow inference can improve WiFi-based multi-person 3D pose estimation without increasing model complexity.
