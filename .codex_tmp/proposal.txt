Abstract 
WiFi-based human pose estimation offers a privacy-preserving and occlusion-resilient alternative to 
cameras. However, translating noisy, low-resolution Channel State Information (CSI) into precise 3D 
skeletons remains a fundamental challenge. Existing architectures heavily rely on complex 
Transformer backbones and one-shot direct regression models like DETR or PETR. These models 
often struggle with signal ambiguity and multipath noise, leading to unstable multi-person pose 
predictions and high computational overhead. To address these limitations, we propose replacing 
direct regression with a generative trajectory learning approach using Rectified Flow. We 
systematically upgrade an established baseline (M0) by introducing Spectral Tokenization to filter 
static noise and a linear-time WiMamba encoder to mitigate the quadratic complexity of Transformers. 
The conventional regression head is then replaced by a Draft-to-Refine Rectified Flow mechanism, 
where an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) guides coarse pose drafts to precise coordinates. 
Preliminary pilot experiments based on a frozen local snapshot indicate that Rectified Flow 
significantly reduces pose errors. We expect our proposed flow-based WiMamba model (M4) to retain 
this generative capability while achieving approximately 160 FPS, halving the parameter count to 
5.8M, and minimizing memory footprint. This research aims to confirm that accurate WiFi pose 
estimation can be optimized through flow-matching refinement without relying on overly complex 
architectures. 
Key Words: WiFi sensing; 3D human pose estimation; channel state information; rectified flow; 
Mamba 
 
1. Introduction 
1.1. Literature review 
Human pose estimation has been widely studied in computer vision, where image-based approaches 
remain dominant for modeling body structure, localizing joints, and reasoning about multiple persons 
[23]. Two paradigms, namely top-down and bottom-up formulations, have shaped this research 
direction, with representative systems such as OpenPose and HRNet demonstrating that strong 
backbones and large annotated datasets can enable highly accurate pose prediction [14], [15]. These 
developments established the fundamental problem formulation later adopted by non-visual sensing 
approaches. However, reliance on cameras raises privacy concerns because visual data may expose 
identity and appearance information, and performance can degrade under poor illumination or heavy 
occlusion. To address these limitations, wireless sensing (including RF and WiFi) has emerged as an 
attractive alternative. Early breakthroughs demonstrated the feasibility of using radio signals for 
through-wall sensing, respiration monitoring, and gesture recognition [16], [17], [18], [21]. Building 
upon these, WiFi sensing specifically utilizes Channel State Information (CSI) to capture human 
motion without visual content, leading to foundational representations like CSI-Net [22] and 
progressive pose estimation pipelines [1]–[7]. Despite these advantages, WiFi-based pose estimation is 
inherently more challenging than image-based methods because wireless signals are indirect, 
low-dimensional, and highly affected by multipath propagation. Consequently, the main challenge is 
not only pose prediction itself, but also learning representations that preserve body structure from 
noisy and indirect CSI measurements, particularly in multi-person 3D settings.

Early studies mainly focused on single-person 2D or 3D pose recovery from CSI, demonstrating that 
wireless signals can support keypoint regression in controlled environments. Representative methods 
such as WiSPPN, WiPose, Winect, and GoPose progressively explored this direction and confirmed 
the feasibility of wireless human pose estimation [3]–[6], [16]. Subsequent benchmark-oriented work, 
including MM-Fi, expanded the research scope by enabling large-scale, non-intrusive multimodal 
human sensing and providing standardized evaluation protocols [7], [19], [20]. Building upon these 
efforts, Person-in-WiFi 3D [1] marked a significant milestone by introducing end-to-end multi-person 
3D pose estimation with a Transformer-based architecture. This work is particularly relevant because 
it formalizes the task definition and provides the practical baseline adopted in this study. Nevertheless, 
most existing pipelines treat WiFi pose estimation as a one-shot direct regression problem [1], [8]. 
Given the ambiguous and indirect nature of CSI signals, one-shot regression to precise 3D coordinates 
may produce structurally inconsistent skeletons, especially when multiple subjects overlap in the 
sensing space. Moreover, to compensate for signal ambiguity, recent approaches increasingly rely on

1.3. Feasibility of research 
The proposed research is highly feasible as it builds upon an established and reproducible baseline. 
The core elements for a controlled empirical study are already in place, including the official 
Person-in-WiFi 3D dataset protocol (see more in Appendix Dataset and Baseline model architecture), 
evaluation metrics, and a functional implementation pipeline [1]. The project does not require massive 
hardware investments, new data collection, or manual annotation campaigns. Instead, it utilizes the 
existing benchmark dataset, which encompasses diverse single- and multi-person scenarios, allowing 
the team to focus entirely on architectural innovation and model optimization. 
Feasibility is further ensured through a structured, progressive ablation design. Rather than comparing 
entirely disconnected architectures, we systematically evaluate individual component upgrades via a 
fixed ladder (M0 to M4): Spectral Tokenization, the WiMamba encoder, and Rectified Flow 
refinement. The provided codebase already supports standardized training, benchmarking, and 
experiment logging. While generative refinement modules may occasionally introduce training 
instability, evaluating them through this strict ablation ladder isolates variables, mitigates technical 
risks, and allows for clear comparisons of accuracy and computational efficiency. 
2. Research Objectives 
Multi-person 3D pose estimation from WiFi Channel State Information (CSI) remains challenging due 
to multipath propagation, measurement noise, and the high computational cost of long-sequence 
modeling. Existing regression-based pipelines often suffer from structural inconsistency under 
ambiguous wireless observations, whereas Transformer-based encoders remain expensive for practical 
deployment. This study aims to develop a generative, computationally efficient framework integrating 
Rectified Flow with a Mamba backbone to improve the accuracy and deployability of WiFi-based 3D 
human pose estimation. We investigate whether Spectral Tokenization and linear-time WiMamba 
encoding can preserve informative CSI representations while reducing sequence complexity relative to 
standard Transformer backbones, and whether a Rectified Flow formulation can provide more 
structurally coherent pose predictions than direct one-shot coordinate regression. 
In addition, this study further seeks to identify the most effective architecture for real-world 
deployment through a controlled comparison of model variants M0-M4 under the fixed 
Person-in-WiFi 3D protocol. The investigation is organized around three research questions: RQ1, 
whether Spectral Tokenization and WiMamba can maintain robust CSI representation at lower 
computational cost; RQ2, whether draft-to-refine flow-based generation improves pose accuracy and 
structural fidelity; and RQ3, which model variant provides the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off in 
practice. By addressing these questions, this research is expected to make three primary contributions:  
1.​ A draft-to-refine Rectified Flow framework tailored to multi-person 3D WiFi pose estimation;  
2.​ A lightweight CSI encoding pipeline combining physics-informed tokenization with 
WiMamba;  
3.​ A rigorously controlled ablation benchmark that disentangles the effects of representation, 
sequence modeling, and generative refinement.

3. Research Scope 
This research focuses on multi-person 3D human pose estimation from WiFi Channel State 
Information under the Person-in-WiFi 3D benchmark setting. The scope is limited to improving the 
existing baseline pipeline through a structured series of model upgrades, rather than introducing new 
sensing hardware or collecting additional datasets. The objective is to evaluate how architectural 
modifications affect prediction accuracy, efficiency, and robustness within a fixed experimental 
protocol. 
CSI Representation: The study investigates improved input representation for WiFi signals. 
Specifically, spectral tokenization is explored as an alternative to the baseline linear projection, aiming 
to produce more motion-aware and noise-robust CSI features. This component focuses on 
strengthening the signal representation stage without altering the dataset or sensing setup. 
Sequence Modeling: The research evaluates sequence modeling strategies for handling long CSI token 
sequences. Transformer-based encoding in the baseline is compared with a WiMamba-based encoder 
to analyze the trade-off between representation capability and computational efficiency. This 
comparison aims to determine whether linear-time sequence modeling can reduce complexity while 
maintaining pose prediction performance. 
Prediction Refinement: To address instability in one-shot regression, we introduce a draft-to-refine 
strategy utilizing rectified-flow correction to progressively improve structural consistency in 
ambiguous multi-person scenarios.​
Model Selection and Evaluation: The final stage compares flow-based Transformer and WiMamba 
variants to identify the most effective configuration. All experiments are conducted using the fixed 
train/test split of the benchmark. Evaluation considers pose accuracy, performance across different 
person counts, runtime efficiency, and qualitative analysis of difficult multi-person cases. 
The research does not include new dataset collection, multimodal sensor fusion, hardware redesign, or 
cross-environment generalization. By restricting the scope to controlled model improvements and 
consistent evaluation, the study aims to provide a clear and focused analysis of how flow-based 
refinement and efficient sequence modeling can improve WiFi-based multi-person 3D pose estimation.

Problem formulation: The task addressed in this proposal is multi-person 3D human pose estimation 
from WiFi channel state information (CSI). In our implementation, WiFi CSI is processed as a 
structured signal representation rather than as a conventional image. The research formulates the 
problem as end-to-end prediction from CSI features to a set of 3D human poses. Since multiple people 
may exist in the same frame, the method must not only regress joint coordinates but also maintain a 
consistent one-to-one relationship between predicted bodies and ground-truth instances. This 
motivates a set-prediction formulation, in which predicted pose instances are matched to ground-truth 
persons in a one-to-one manner, following DETR/PETR-style design principles [8], [9].

baseline projects CSI into latent tokens, models global interactions with

Ablation-based evaluation strategy: To study the contribution of each component systematically, the 
method is evaluated through a staged ladder from M0 to M4. M0 is Person-in-WiFi 3D. M1 is 
Person-in-WiFi 3D + Spectral Tokens. M2 is Person-in-WiFi 3D + Mamba. M3 is Draft-to-Refine 
Rectified Flow with Transformer. M4 is Draft-to-Refine Rectified Flow with Mamba. This staged 
structure is central to the evidence strategy of the proposal because it allows each design decision to be 
interpreted through controlled comparison rather than isolated claim-making. 
 
Evaluation Protocol: To ensure a fair and credible ablation study, all variants (M0 to M4) will be 
evaluated using the official Person-in-WiFi 3D train/test split. The configurations will share the same 
optimization budget, augmentation strategies, and evaluation scripts. To mitigate statistical variance 
and confirm that performance margins are architecturally significant rather than seed-dependent, the 
final reported metrics will be averaged across multiple random seeds. 
Furthermore, while prior works rely heavily on MPJPE, this metric alone does not fully capture 
structural deformations. To validate the hypothesis that Rectified Flow improves structural stability in 
crowded scenes, our evaluation will be expanded to include Procrustes Aligned MPJPE (PA-MPJPE) 
to measure scale/rotation-invariant structural fidelity, and Bone Length Consistency (BLC) to quantify 
anatomical violations (e.g., unnatural limb stretching). We utilize BLC to prove a core advantage of 
our approach: the generative ODE trajectory of Rectified Flow inherently learns and preserves valid 
human anatomical distributions, achieving structural coherence without requiring explicit, manually 
tuned bone loss functions. Efficiency benchmarks, including FPS, parameter count, and peak memory, 
will also be systematically logged.

6. Expected Results

and practical. In terms of methodology, the project is expected to show that refinement-based 
prediction is a viable alternative to one-shot regression under the benchmark setting. The intended 
result is a cleaner, more robust pipeline that integrates physics-informed Spectral Tokenization, 
linear-time WiMamba sequence modeling, and a draft-to-refine correction process within the official 
benchmark setting [1], [10], [11]. In this sense, the expected contribution is not merely a new 
architectural configuration, but a fundamental simplification of multi-person 3D pose estimation from 
ambiguous WiFi signals.

Experimentally, the project is expected to deliver a structured ablation analysis quantifying the 
contribution of each architectural component. As indicated by the preliminary benchmark (Table 2), 
transitioning from the baseline direct regression model (M0) to the Transformer-based flow model 
(M3) reduces the Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) from 169.34 mm to 151.99 mm. This 
highlights the effectiveness of generative Rectified Flow in resolving the structural instability of 
one-shot predictions. 
However, generative refinement inherently introduces computational overhead. The proposed final 
model, Draft-to-Refine Rectified Flow with Mamba(M4), addresses this bottleneck by substituting the 
Transformer backbone with the linear-time WiMamba encoder. While M4 incurs a marginal accuracy 
penalty of ~7 mm compared to M3, it introduces a highly effective architectural trade-off.  
Specifically, M4 achieves the highest inference speed (159.85 FPS) and reduces the baseline 
parameter count by over 50% (to 5.83M). Most notably, M4 minimizes peak memory consumption by 
an order of magnitude (from 155.60 MB in M0 to just 27.02 MB). This drastic memory efficiency is 
achieved by stripping away the heavy bipartite matching and multi-head cross-attention decoders of 
standard DETR-style heads (M0/M1/M2), replacing them with our streamlined ODE flow matching 
head. To provide a holistic view of these architectural trade-offs, Figure 4 illustrates a multi-metric 
performance comparison of all variants. The expected outcomes will demonstrate that while M3 
establishes the upper bound for accuracy, Draft-to-Refine Rectified Flow with Mamba(M4) delivers

collected with seven volunteers performing eight daily actions—including reaching out, sitting down, 
and walking—across three distinct locations: an office, a classroom, and a corridor. Figure 6 displays

2.​ Baseline model architecture: 
The Person-in-WiFi 3D system introduces the Wi-Fi Pose Transformer, an end-to-end framework 
designed to estimate multi-person 3D poses directly from Wi-Fi signals. Unlike traditional methods 
that rely on 3D keypoint heatmaps and part affinity fields, this architecture treats pose estimation as a 
set prediction task, significantly improving storage efficiency and processing speed. The model is 
composed of three primary modules: a CSI Feature Encoder, a Pose Decoder, and a Refine Decoder.  
The architecture of Person-in-WiFi 3D is illustrated in Figure 7, which utilizes a transformer-based 
structure to process flattened CSI tokens. The CSI Feature Encoder consists of six layers, each 
utilizing multi-head self-attention to extract global context from 180 spatial-temporal tokens enriched 
with learnable embeddings. Following the encoder, the Pose Decoder uses 100 learnable queries 
across three layers to predict initial 3D body coordinates and confidence scores. A unique feature of 
this decoder is the prediction of relative offsets at each layer rather than a full pose, ensuring the 
cumulative sum converges accurately to the final coordinates. Finally, the Refine Decoder employs 
three additional layers to fine-tune the keypoint coordinates of the detected bodies, further reducing 
localization errors. To enable end-to-end training, a set-based Hungarian loss is implemented, ensuring 
unique bipartite matching between predicted poses and ground-truth individuals

3.​ Detail qualitative WiFi pose samples: 
Figure A1, A2, and A3 presents selected detailed qualitative inspection across different subject counts 
including 1-person, 2-person, and 3-person settings. These visualizations provide an intuitive 
assessment that complements the quantitative metrics. As the number of subjects increases, the 
limitations of the baseline direct regression model (M0) become apparent: multipath interference in 
crowded environments leads to distorted and anatomically implausible skeleton predictions, which can 
be observed in the collapsed gray poses in the multi-person cases. 
 
In contrast, the flow-based variants (M3 and M4) demonstrate more stable and coherent pose 
reconstruction. By progressively refining predictions along the ODE trajectory, both models preserve 
structural consistency and reduce limb discontinuities. The visual comparison further indicates that the 
lightweight Mamba-based variant (M4) achieves pose quality comparable to the heavier 
Transformer-based variant (M3).
